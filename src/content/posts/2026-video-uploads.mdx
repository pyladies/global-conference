If you are performing in [PyJam](https://conference.pyladies.com/2026-pyjam-track-announcement/),
recording a highlight video for your PyLadies chapter, or giving any other pre-recorded session
at PyLadiesCon 2026, you can now send us your video.
It goes through the [PyLadiesCon portal](https://portal.pyladies.com), from your own session
page, and the deadline is **21 November 2026**.

When we [opened the portal to speakers](https://conference.pyladies.com/2026-speaker-portal/)
we said that uploading recordings was something we were still building. We are happy to
announce that it is now open. Let us walk you through what it looks like, and what to expect
after you press Upload.

## What it replaces

In past years we shared a Google Drive folder with each speaker. It worked, mostly, and it had
problems we never liked. You needed a Google account to upload to it, so people without one
were left out or had to find a workaround. Once your file was in a shared folder, keeping track
of it was on us: which version was the latest, whether anyone had checked its length, and where
the edited version went when it came back. And a Drive folder lets you upload a file, but it
tells nobody what the file is. There is no place to say "this is the transcript, reviewed by the
team" or "this is the final cut, approved by the speaker", so we all relied on file names like
`transcript-v1`, `transcript-v2-final`, `transcript-v2-final-approved`, and hoped everyone read
them the same way.

The portal does away with all of that. You sign in with whatever email address you gave us, and
upload from your session page. Every file carries what it is and which version it is, the portal
checks the length, and it shows you what the team does with it next. It not only lets us share
files with each other securely, it helps all of us understand and keep track of where your
session is.

## How the upload works

Open **Speaking**, then **My sessions**, and choose your session. At the top is a tile for your
video; while there is nothing in it, it says so, and shows the length limit for your kind of
session. A PyJam performance and a chapter highlight have different limits, and the page shows
yours.

![The top of a performer's session page: a tile that says the video is not uploaded yet, next to the to-dos and the slot](/images/posts/2026-video-upload-tile.png "Your session page, before the upload")

*Your session page before the upload. The names are practice data.*

Choose **Upload video**, pick the file, and choose **Upload**. The file goes straight to our
storage in pieces, a few at a time, so a file of several gigabytes is fine. If your connection
drops, nothing is lost: come back, choose the same file again, and it carries on from where it
stopped.

When it is done you see version 1, and within a moment its **length against the limit**: a green
bar if it fits, a red one with a plain message if it does not. Performances over the limit are
the thing we most often had to cut by hand in past years, so now the portal tells you before we
have to.

![The Files tab of a session page: the video card with the length bar and the upload panel](/images/posts/2026-video-upload-panel.png "Your video, with its length checked against the limit")

*Your video after the upload: the length is checked against the limit, and a new take becomes the next version. Practice data.*

Uploaded your video, but now want to send a different one, because the first was too long, or
you forgot to mention something? **Replace video** makes it version 2, and we always work from
your newest one. Earlier versions stay listed under the card in case you want one back. And if
you simply decide you do not want to share a video with us anymore, you can delete it: you have
full control over the files you submit to us, and deleting removes every version from our
systems and our storage, so download a copy first.

## What happens after you upload

Your upload ticks **Upload your performance video** on your checklist, and tells your liaison,
the organizer looking after you, at once. Then the third list on your checklist, **What we're
preparing for your video**, takes over. You can watch your video move through the team's work:
the quality and length check, the transcript and its review, translations, the title card and
final cut, and the YouTube publish scheduled for your slot.

![The third list on a performer's checklist, What we're preparing for your video: the intro and outro, the quality review, the length check and the transcript ticked, the transcript review, the translation, the title card and final cut, and the YouTube publish, each with its date](/images/posts/2026-video-checklist.png "What we're preparing for your video: the team's work on your recording, step by step")

*The team's work on your video, on your checklist. Practice data.*

A word on transcripts. The portal makes the first draft of your transcript itself: it runs
[Whisper](https://github.com/openai/whisper), the open source speech recognition model (the
`small` model, through faster-whisper), on our own worker, and a person on the team reviews the
draft. Your recording is not sent to an outside transcription service.

## Files from the team

Sharing goes the other way too. The poster and social cards for your session, the transcript
for you to check, and the final cut of your video all come back to you through the same page,
under **Files from the team** on the **Files** tab. Each file has a title that says what it is,
a **Preview** that opens it right there, and a **Download** button.

![Files from the team on a performer's session page: the final cut, a transcript and a poster, each with a Preview fold and a Download button](/images/posts/2026-video-team-files.png "Files from the team: the poster, the transcript and the final cut")

*What the team shared, on your Files tab. Practice data.*

You do not need to keep checking. The portal sends you at most one email a day, the same one
that reminds you of to-dos coming due, and when the team has shared something new it lists the
files and points you to your session page. The email never carries the files or links to them:
the portal only ever hands out links that expire a minute after you click, so there is no link
floating around a thread that anyone could follow later.

When the final cut arrives, a to-do opens: **Approve the final cut**. Watch it, and if you are
happy for it to go out, tick it. If something needs fixing, tell us what and where, and we will
fix it before anything is published.

## Your files and who can see them

Your video and the team's files live in private storage. There is no public link to any of
them. You can fetch your own video and whatever the team has shared with you; the organizers and
your liaison can fetch everything on your session; nobody else can. The team's reviewer notes on
a file stay with the team. Files are kept until someone deletes them, and you can delete your
own video yourself, or ask us to.

The portal is open source, so you do not have to take our word for any of this: the code is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/pyladies/pyladiescon-portal).

## Getting started

1. Record your video to the specs in the
   [Video guide](https://conference.pyladies.com/docs/speakers/video_guide/): landscape, MP4
   preferred, and within the length for your session (up to 10 minutes for PyJam; see the
   [PyJam performer guide](https://conference.pyladies.com/docs/speakers/pyjam_guide/)).
2. Sign in to the [portal](https://portal.pyladies.com), open **Speaking**, **My sessions**, and
   your session.
3. Choose **Upload video**, pick the file, and choose **Upload**.
4. Check the length bar, and you are done. Keep an eye on your email for the files we send back.

The deadline for your video is **21 November 2026**. If you cannot make it, or cannot use the
upload for any reason, reply to any email from the portal and tell us; we would rather find
another way than have you stuck.
