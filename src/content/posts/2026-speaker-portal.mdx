If you have proposed a workshop or a PyJam performance for PyLadiesCon 2026, or we have invited you
to lead a workshop, join a panel or perform, you have probably noticed something: we ask you to
sign in to the [PyLadiesCon portal](https://portal.pyladies.com) and share your details there,
instead of sending us a form or an email.

We want to explain why, because it is a change from how we have run session logistics in past
years, and we think it makes the experience better for you and for us.

## What it replaces

In previous editions, information about the people on our program lived in a lot of places. Your bio was in Pretalx. The
schedule was in a spreadsheet, and so was the record of who had been sent which email. There were
forms for this, and a long email thread whenever something changed. Every organizer had a slightly
different version of the truth, and everyone was asked for the same details more than once.

We also shared a Google Drive folder for people to upload their videos. That came with a problem
we want to own: Google Drive only lets people with a Google account upload to a shared folder, so
people without one were left out or had to find a workaround. We recognize that limitation, and it
is not an issue with the portal. Use whatever email address you like, whether it is a work,
consulting or personal address. We only ask that it is a primary one that you will check, because
that is where we will write to you.

The portal replaces all of those with one place.

## A journey that is yours

When you accept an invitation, or when we approve your proposal, you land on your own dashboard:
your session, where it stands, and what needs your attention next.

![Speaker dashboard showing a confirmed workshop, task counts and the next to-do that is due](/images/posts/2026-speaker-portal-dashboard.png "Your dashboard after accepting an invitation")

*Your dashboard after accepting an invitation, with your session, its status and what is due next. The names are practice data.*

From there you get a personal checklist. It shows:

- **What we need from you**, with due dates in your own timezone: check your session title and
  summary, add your bio and headshot, read the guide for your session, join Discord, register for the
  conference, share your slides, and book a tech check.
- **What we're doing to prepare your session**: onboarding, the Discord channel and role, promo
  materials, scheduling, the confirmation of your slot, and the day-of reminder. You can see who
  on the team has each item and whether it is waiting on you or on us.

![The speaker checklist, with the speaker's to-dos above a list of what the team is preparing](/images/posts/2026-speaker-portal-checklist.png "Your checklist: what you need to do, and what the team is doing to prepare your session")

*Your checklist: what you need to do, and what the team is doing to prepare your session. Practice data.*

You also get reminder emails as due dates get closer, so you do not have to keep the page open.

You enter your details once. Your bio, photo, links and session details live on your profile, and
we use them from there, instead of asking you to send them again for the website, the schedule and
the social media cards.

## Your journey as a presenter is more than the proposal

Sending your session details is the start, not the end. If we had given you a Google Form to fill
in, that is where your journey would have ended. That is what a form is for: information intake. It
does not remind you what is due, show you what the team is doing, confirm your slot with you, share
your promo materials, or tell us when something is stuck.

Between now and the conference there is a program to build, materials to prepare, a schedule to
confirm, and people to introduce you. That is a lot of small steps for both of us to keep track of,
and it is easy to lose one.

That is why your checklist stays open until the end of the conference. It lets you and the
organizers see the same picture of how far along things are, and it lets us notice when something
is stuck long before the day.

If you are performing in PyJam, or giving a pre-recorded session, the same goes for your video.
Uploading recordings through the portal is something we are still building. When it opens we will
email everyone it affects and add the upload to your checklist. We are designing it so that your
files go to storage that only you and the team can reach, rather than a shared link that floats
around in a thread.

## Your information

The portal stores what you give us: your name and email, bio, an optional photo, your timezone and
links, and the details of your session. It has no third-party analytics or advertising trackers.
Like most sites it does load fonts, icons and one script library from public content delivery
networks.

The portal itself is open source, built with Python and Django, and the code is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/pyladies/pyladiescon-portal). You do not have to take our word for what
it does with your information: you can read it, and if you spot something, or want to help improve
it, issues and pull requests are welcome.

## Getting started

- **Want to give a workshop or a PyJam performance?** Head to the [propose page](https://portal.pyladies.com/speakers/propose/). If
  you volunteered with PyLadiesCon before, you already have an account and can sign in with it.
- **Were you invited?** Open the email from us and choose the accept button. It signs you in and
  creates your account, and you do not need a password.
